How it works

Updated 8 October 2026

Irpin City is an iPhone app with a small server behind it. Almost everything runs on the phone; the server does only the things a phone cannot do alone: keep a booking request until a staff member answers it, push a notification while the app is closed, and publish updated reference data without a new App Store release. This page describes exactly what runs where, which data goes through which part, and what each screen and service does.

At a glance

  • The phone does the work. The map, building heights, timetables, the wallet, utility calculations, routes and navigation are computed on the iPhone. The wallet (documents, meters, cars, payments, the emergency card) is stored only there, optionally locked with Face ID.
  • Three small services run on one server, api.munister.com.ua, behind HTTPS: the service-centre (CNAP) service, the push service and a static folder of signed reference data.
  • Open sources are asked directly by the phone: OpenStreetMap map data, the DTEK outage schedule published as open data, the Ukrzaliznytsia booking site. The developer's server is not in between.
  • No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no third-party trackers. The server never asks who you are; the only personal data it ever receives is what you type into an appointment request.

The service-centre (CNAP) service

The service sits between the app and the state sites. It does three jobs.

1. A catalogue of services. The state "Guide to public services" publishes a register of 516 services. The service reads it once a day, keeps the services the centre provides, and the staff choose the 75 most requested, with their group, queue and order. The app shows each service with its documents, term and fee, and a "Book" button. If the state site is down, yesterday's copy is shown.

2. Free time in the queue. For a service tied to an electronic queue, the service reads the free half-hour slots live from the Diia queue site (cached for two minutes) and offers only those. If the queue site does not answer, the last seen time is shown and marked as possibly out of date.

3. Booking through the Diia electronic queue. This is the default. The app shows free times from the CNAP queue, checks the chosen time once more and opens the queue website in a built-in window, where you sign in through id.gov.ua (only the first time: the sign-in is remembered on the phone). The ticket is created by the queue itself, so CNAP staff see it in their own system, not in a separate program. The app reads the ticket number from the confirmation page itself, and moved, cancelled or elsewhere-made bookings from your account; all of it stays on the phone. If the confirmation is not recognised, an “I've booked” button saves the booking by hand.

4. Appointment requests without signing in to Diia. These work only when CNAP switches on requests from the app. You choose a day and a time, type your name and phone, and agree to the processing. A staff member sees the request in an admin panel and confirms it with an exact time, proposes another time, or declines it with a reason. Auto-confirmation is on by default: while the chosen time has room, the request is confirmed at once. A request is not a queue ticket until the centre confirms it, and the app says so.

How a request moves

  • New → In work → Confirmed → In the hall → Done. Side exits: Declined (by the centre, with a reason), Cancelled (by you), No-show, Expired. The server rejects any move that is not allowed.
  • Another time. If your time does not suit, the centre proposes its own. In the app you answer "Accept" (confirmed at once) or "Decline" (cancelled). The proposed time stays reserved while you think.
  • At the centre. On the day, tap "I'm here" (not earlier than 30 minutes before). The centre has its own electronic queue, so the app shows the ticket number it gives you and your visit status. Calling people by window through this service is off by default and can be switched on where there is no electronic queue; then the window number appears in the app and on the hall board.
  • Result. After the visit the centre can mark the result "will be later (date)", "ready" or "issued"; the app tells you when it is ready.
  • Rating. After the visit you can rate it from 1 to 5; the rating and comment are shown to the centre.
  • Reception pauses during an air raid alert in the app, the admin panel and the hall board.

For the staff: the admin panel

  • Tabs by stage with a wait timer (red after four hours), search, free times for the day, a day schedule by half hours with print and CSV, and desk booking for people who come in or phone.
  • Roles: an operator handles requests; an administrator also manages the catalogue, announcements, halls, reception hours, closed days and staff.
  • Automation: auto-confirm, assign a new request to the least loaded operator, mark no-shows after the day.
  • Overview charts, a monitoring page (staff online, the app's pulse per minute, health of the server and of the queue site) and a CSV report without names or phones.

Protection

  • Against spam: limits per address in nginx (reading 180 requests a minute, requests 60), limits in the service (20 requests an hour and 60 a day per address), three open requests per phone number, two requests per slot in one queue, a block list.
  • Signed requests: the app signs requests with a key built into it. It filters out scripts; it is not a secret.
  • Encryption at rest: files with personal data are encrypted with AES-256-GCM; the keys are derived from one master secret that lives only in a root-only file.
  • Staff: personal logins, passwords stored only as scrypt hashes, a lock after 8 failed attempts, optional two-step sign-in (TOTP and one-time reserve codes), a departed employee's access is switched off at once.
  • A tamper-evident log: every action is recorded in a hash chain, so an unnoticed edit breaks it.
  • Short retention and erasure: requests are deleted 30 days after the visit date; on request, name, phone and notes are erased at once.

The push service

It does two things: watches public sources and delivers notifications through Apple's service (APNs).

  • Air raid alerts. Every 15 seconds the service reads the state of the Irpin community from the open alert mirror (region 706). When an alert starts or ends, it sends a time-sensitive notification to every subscribed phone. If the server has just started in the middle of a long alert, it does not send a late "alert" that would only scare.
  • Outages that are not on the schedule. DTEK schedules do not know about accidents, and DTEK publishes no open feed of them (its site is closed to automated reading). So the app lets residents report: “No power” (accepted only while the schedule says there should be power, so a planned outage never counts) and “Power is on”. The push service groups reports by a square of about 1 km and its neighbours. When three different phones report in the same area within 15 minutes, a “possible outage” opens and the other residents of that area who turned on “Outages nearby” get a notification; when two report that power is back, they get the all-clear. These are residents' reports, not official data, and the app says so. A phone counts as one vote, and only phones known to the service can vote.
  • Kyiv metro. Once a minute it reads the public channel of the metro and pushes new service changes.
  • Your CNAP request. When the centre confirms a request, proposes another time, declines it, calls you to a desk or marks the result ready, the CNAP service asks the push service to notify the phone attached to that request. The text contains the service, the day and time, never your name or phone.
  • Lock Screen. For a confirmed visit, the app starts a Live Activity two hours before; the CNAP service updates it through a separate Live Activity token. It ends after the visit.
  • Without push the app still checks alerts in the background from public sources, but iOS runs that at irregular times, so it can be late.
  • Notifications are a convenience. They can be late or missing; always rely on sirens and official channels.

Reference data without a release

Tariffs (and in time other reference data) are published as small files with a signed manifest. The app checks it at launch, in the background and on "Settings → Data → Update now". It accepts a file only if the signature is valid (the public key is built into the app), the version is newer than what it has, the checksum matches and the content passes the same checks as the built-in copy. Otherwise it keeps what it had. The screen says "current as of" a date and warns when tariffs are over 30 days old.

Screens and services

Home

  • Live cards over a night map: the next train to Kyiv with a countdown and the fare; how many venues are open now; the nearest shelter with distance and address; your power queue and the next change.
  • An alert banner when there is an air raid alert; the writer of the day; a short piece of town history.

Booking

  • A four-step booking flow for a service-centre appointment, then "My visits": status, countdown, ticket with a QR code, "I'm here", answer to a proposed time, rating, and the result.

Services

  • The service-centre card with its live open or closed status, the catalogue of 75 services and the register of 516, 12 life situations (registration, property, sole trader, birth of a child, IDP status, pension…) with a document checklist.
  • The wallet (full screen), a phone book of 72 numbers (emergency, offices, utilities, your own), 451 venues, 61 residential complexes, 66 sights, 50 walking and bike routes, Literary Irpin (15 writers) and the town's history.

Transport

  • Trains from Irpin with a countdown, reminders and a ticket link with the stations and date filled in; minibuses 392, 395, 100, 6616 and others to Kyiv, Bucha and Hostomel with routes and stops; the Kyiv metro map and service notices; cycle path segments on the map with "ride it" navigation.

Map

  • A night map on OpenStreetMap: buildings in 3D by floor count, shelters, stops, venues with an "open now" filter, cycle paths and search by street or venue.

Wallet

  • Home, parcel locker, transport, medicines and the emergency card; documents with expiry reminders (30, 7 and 1 day); cars with insurance and inspection dates; meters with readings, consumption and a chart; utilities: an estimate of the monthly total from your readings and the current tariffs for electricity, gas, water, heating, waste and your own services, the reading window, payment deadline and a six-month chart; a "needs attention" block.
  • Backup: an encrypted file with a password you choose (AES-256-GCM, key derived with PBKDF2). You pick the sections, save the file where you like and can restore it on a new phone; a restore can be undone. The password is stored nowhere and cannot be recovered.

Widgets and the Lock Screen

  • Widgets: air raid alert, the next train from Irpin, power on or off for your queue, your next service-centre visit (the ticket number is hidden on a locked screen), and the writer of the day.
  • Live Activities: the service-centre visit and a countdown to a train you choose to follow.

Settings

  • Notifications (alerts and all-clear, metro, service-centre request status, the visit on the Lock Screen, train reminders, fact of the day), the data screen, appearance and language, map and train options, privacy and legal texts.

What it is not

Irpin City is an independent app. It is not an official application of the city council, DTEK, Ukrzaliznytsia, Diia or any other institution, and it does not replace sirens or official warning systems. Timetables, tariffs and opening hours come from open sources: check before you travel or pay.
