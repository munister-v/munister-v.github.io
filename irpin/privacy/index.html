This policy explains what happens to your data when you use Irpin City, an iPhone app published by Vyacheslaw Munister. It covers the app itself, the optional push notification service, the CNAP services and appointment request service, the problem reports service, and the outside services the app connects to. It does not cover websites you open from the app: their own policies apply there.

The Android app has a section of its own: Android app.

Summary

  • There are no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking.
  • What you enter in the app (documents, meters, payments, your home, the emergency card) is stored only on your iPhone. The developer never receives it.
  • When you book CNAP, the city’s Administrative Service Centre, through the Diia electronic queue, you enter your details on the state queue website, not in the app: the developer does not receive them.
  • You hand over your name and phone number to the developer in only two cases: an appointment request to CNAP and, if you yourself choose “I want a reply by phone”, a problem report about the city. Only CNAP or city council staff see them. A request is deleted 30 days after the date you chose, or at once if you cancel it; the phone number in a report is deleted 30 days after it is closed, or at once if you withdraw it.
  • Your location is used only on the phone. The exception is a problem report: then the point you placed with the pin on the map goes to the server, not the phone’s position.
  • A problem report (photos, description, place) is seen by Irpin City Council staff and, once approved, by other users, without your name, phone or any sign of who the author is. You can flag someone else’s report.
  • If you turn on push notifications for air raid alerts or metro service changes, the developer’s server stores an anonymous push token for your device. Nothing else.
  • To show maps, timetables, alerts and the power schedule, the app requests public data from outside services. They see your IP address, as any website would.
  • You can delete everything at any time by turning the notifications off and deleting the app.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Vyacheslaw Munister, the publisher of the app. For any question about this policy or your data, write to munister@outlook.com. You will receive a reply in English or Ukrainian, usually within a few days and no later than 30 days.

What “personal data” means here

Personal data is any information that identifies you or could be linked to you, such as your name, address, location, documents or a device identifier. Technical data that cannot be linked to you (for example, which map tiles were requested) is not personal data on its own.

The request is seen only by Irpin CNAP staff, who sign in to a staff page with personal passwords, and by the developer as the party maintaining the server. Every staff action on a request is recorded in a log. The data is shared with no one else and used for nothing but the appointment.

The server does not send your name and phone back to the app: they remain on the phone only because you typed them there. The server reveals a request’s status only against a secret key stored on your iPhone. The QR code and barcode on the ticket hold only the request number, with no name or phone. The ticket sheet (PDF) is created on the phone and goes nowhere until you share it yourself.

This service does not record your IP address. To stop abuse, the server counts how many requests arrive from one address, but the address itself is stored nowhere: memory and the event log keep only an anonymised mark from which the address cannot be recovered. CNAP staff can block a phone number that files false requests; such a number is kept in the block list until it is unblocked.

Booking by phone or at the desk. If you contact CNAP without the app, a staff member may enter you into the same service themselves. Before doing so they ask for your consent to CNAP keeping your name and phone number for this appointment; without consent no booking is created. The same data is kept, for the same purpose and for the same period: 30 days after the visit date. Such a booking does not appear in the app. To cancel it or have it deleted sooner, contact CNAP or write to munister@outlook.com.

Staff may add a short internal note to a request for their own use (for example, “call back after 14:00”). It is deleted together with the request; you can get a copy of it on request, like the rest of the request data.

It is a problem report, not an official appeal under the Law of Ukraine “On Citizens’ Appeals”, and it does not replace 112, 101, 102, 103 or 104.

Who sees what. The full card (description, photos, address, history, and the phone number if you gave one) is seen by city council staff who sign in to the staff page with personal passwords and two-step sign-in; each of their actions is logged, and showing a phone number is logged as a separate entry. At first only you see the report. Once a staff member approves it, other users of the app see the category, status, address, description, photos and number of supporters, with the point rounded to about 10 m. Your name, phone, identifier and key are never shown to other users. Please do not photograph people or licence plates and do not put phone numbers or email addresses in the description: staff may remove such content.

Moderation. Every report by someone else has a “Report” button. Three flags hide a report until staff check it. Staff can hide or remove content, reject a report and block sending from a given app installation. You can write about abuse to munister@outlook.com. There are no private messages, chat or comments in the app.

Report data on the server’s disk is encrypted. This service does not record IP addresses: for rate limiting only an anonymous mark stays in memory, from which the address cannot be recovered.

Booking through the Diia electronic queue

Normally you book CNAP through the state electronic queue (cherga.diia.gov.ua) that CNAP uses for appointments. The app shows the free times, checks the chosen time on the queue website once more and opens the booking page in a built-in window, like a browser. There you sign in through id.gov.ua (Diia.Signature, BankID or a qualified e-signature), and everything you enter there goes only to the queue website: the developer does not see it, and the Diia privacy policy applies. The sign-in is kept in the built-in window on your iPhone (as in a browser) so you don't have to sign in again; you can erase it from the queue window menu: “Sign out of the queue account”.

So as not to ask you unnecessarily, the app reads, on the phone, the confirmation page and your account page on the queue website: the ticket number, day, time and whether the booking was cancelled. This way it saves the ticket itself, notices moved or cancelled bookings and shows bookings made on the website or at the terminal. It stores on your iPhone the service, the date and time of the visit, the ticket number and the ticket barcode, to remind you an hour before and to show the ticket at the desk, as well as the name shown in the queue account, to tell bookings apart when several people sign in on the same phone. Neither the page contents nor this information are sent anywhere; you can remove a booking in the Booking tab. The app offers a request through the developer’s server (section above) only when CNAP has switched on requests from the app.

CNAP services catalogue

The list of services, free times in the queue and taken times also come from the developer’s server. These requests contain nothing about you. When you search for a service, the server receives the query text and counts how many times a word was searched that day, with digits removed, no longer than 40 characters and with no link to you or your device. This helps CNAP see which services people look for and do not find.

Location

Location is optional. If you allow it (“While Using the App”), the app uses it to show where you are, to find the nearest shelters, stops and venues, and to guide you along a route. The position is processed on the phone. It is not stored, not sent to the developer and not shared with any service listed above.

Exception: problem reports. When you send a report, the point you chose with the pin on the map goes to the server. The phone’s position is not sent; the point matches it only if you chose “My location” and did not move the pin. The address is filled in on the phone from the building data inside the app; no outside address service is called for this.

Route navigation. Navigation on a walking or bike route works while the app is open on screen. The app does not receive your location in the background.

“No network” and “I’m OK”. Distances, the direction to a shelter and the compass are computed on the phone. The “I’m OK” message with a link to your place is composed on the phone and sent only when you yourself tap Send in Messages or another app; the developer does not receive it. Siri, Spotlight and Shortcuts answer from data on the phone.

Reporting a problem

“Settings → Report a problem or an idea” opens a new email in your own mail app, addressed to the developer. It contains the text you write, a screenshot only if you attach one, and, unless you switch it off, technical details: app version, iOS version, device model, language and which permissions are granted. Nothing is sent until you press Send yourself. Emails are kept in the developer’s mailbox for as long as needed to answer and fix the issue, and you can ask for them to be deleted.

How data is used

Data is used only to provide the features you see in the app. It is not used for advertising, profiling, analytics or training of any model, and it is not sold or rented to anyone. The app does not track you across apps or websites, as defined by Apple’s App Tracking Transparency.

Sharing with third parties

The developer does not share personal data with third parties, except in three cases. The first is the push token, which Apple receives in order to deliver notifications; Apple processes it under its own privacy policy and the Apple Developer Program terms. The second is an appointment request: it is received by the CNAP of the Irpin City Council Executive Committee, because that is what you submit it for. The third is a problem report: it is received by Irpin City Council staff and, once approved, is also seen by other users of the app (see “Problem reports about the city”). The outside services listed above receive requests directly from your device, not from the developer, and only the technical data needed to answer them.

Security

Data on the device is protected by iOS encryption and, if you choose, by the wallet lock. The push service accepts connections only over HTTPS, requires an app key and stores nothing beyond what is listed above. The request service also works only over HTTPS; CNAP staff sign in with personal passwords (the server keeps only their hashes), every action goes into a log protected against unnoticed edits, and a departed employee’s access is switched off at once. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, but the less data exists, the less can be exposed, which is why the app keeps almost everything on your phone.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal data from children, and it does not collect personal data from anyone except the push token described above and an appointment request that a person submits themselves.

International transfers

The outside services and Apple may process requests on servers outside Ukraine and the European Union. The developer does not transfer your personal data abroad, except that the push token is delivered to Apple. Appointment requests are stored on a server in Ukraine.

Your rights

Under the Law of Ukraine “On Personal Data Protection” and, if you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, the GDPR, you have the right to:

  • know whether your data is processed and get a copy of it;
  • have inaccurate data corrected;
  • have your data deleted;
  • object to or restrict processing, and withdraw consent at any time;
  • complain to a supervisory authority: in Ukraine, the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights; in the EU, the data protection authority of your country.

Apart from appointment requests and problem reports (the phone number, if you gave one), the developer keeps no data that identifies you, so a request can usually be met by the steps in “Retention and deletion”. To get a copy of a request, correct it or delete it, write to munister@outlook.com with the request number (IR-…): that lets us find it without asking for more. Processing a request rests on your consent; you can withdraw it by cancelling the request. You can withdraw a problem report in the app (“My reports → Withdraw”): the text, photos and phone number are erased from the server at once. To get a copy of a report or have it deleted, write to munister@outlook.com with its number (RP-…).

Changes to this policy

If the policy changes, the new version will be published at munister.com.ua/irpin/privacy and in the app (“Settings → About”) with a new date. If a change means the app starts collecting new data, it will ask for your consent before doing so.

Android app

The rest of this policy describes the iPhone app. For data in the beta of Irpin City for Android, installed from an APK file on this site, read this section. “Who is responsible”, “How data is used”, “Children”, “Your rights”, “Changes to this policy” and “Contact” apply to it too.

It has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. It has no push notifications either: the phone itself shows reminders. The Android version does not send CNAP appointment requests through the developer’s server, or entrance marks. The APK file is downloaded from GitHub, which sees your IP address during the download.

What stays on the phone

The wallet, “My home”, the emergency card, the go-bag, your phone numbers, the visits you wrote down, favourites, settings and report drafts are kept in the app’s own storage on the phone. The developer does not receive this data. The exception is what you yourself send in a problem report.

  • Protection. The data is protected by Android’s own means; the app does not encrypt it separately. If you turn on the wallet lock and the phone has a screen lock, opening the wallet needs your PIN, pattern or biometrics. Android checks them; the app receives no biometric data. Screens behind the lock do not appear in screenshots or in the recent apps list.
  • Backups. Android backup is switched off for the app, so this data does not go into a cloud backup. Some manufacturers still copy app data when you move from an old phone to a new one.
  • Deletion. Uninstall the app or clear its data in Android settings. Withdraw any reports you have sent first: their keys are deleted along with the data.

What the app connects to

  • Developer’s server (api.munister.com.ua), alerts and power. To show the air raid alert status and the outage schedule: in the open app, from widgets and for power reminders, including in the background. The request contains nothing about you.
  • siren.pp.ua and GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com). Backup sources for the alert and the schedule when the developer’s server does not answer. For the alert, the region code of the Irpin community is sent; for the schedule, nothing about you.
  • Developer’s server, CNAP. The service catalogue and free queue times. The queue number is sent; search text is not.
  • Developer’s server, problem reports. The list of problem types, the reports map, your and other people’s reports. What exactly is sent is described below.
  • OpenFreeMap (tiles.openfreemap.org). While a map is on screen: the area being shown.
  • City council website (imr.gov.ua). When you open the council’s appeal form or the page with officials’ photos. The website receives what you yourself enter on it.

Each such request, like any internet connection, reveals your IP address and ordinary technical details to the service. Requests to the developer’s server also name the app and the platform (Android).

The council’s appeal form opens in a built-in window, like a browser: the council website receives what you enter, and its rules apply there. That website’s cookies stay on the phone until you clear the app’s data.

Links to the Diia queue, Ukrzaliznytsia, DTEK and other websites, maps, calls and the Share button open the browser or other apps. Their own rules apply there.

Problem reports

The section “Problem reports about the city” above applies to them: what is sent, how long it is kept, who sees it and how moderation works. The differences on Android:

  • No push token is sent: this version has no notifications about report status.
  • The report’s secret key is not in the Keychain but in the app’s own storage on the phone. If you uninstall the app or clear its data, you can no longer withdraw the report from the phone: write to munister@outlook.com and give the number (RP-…).
  • You take photos with the system camera or choose them in the system photo picker. The app has no camera permission and no access to your whole gallery. Before sending, it re-encodes each photo and removes the metadata.
  • To show similar reports before you send yours, the server receives the point where the pin stands and the problem type. The reports map sends the bounds of the area you are viewing.
  • The phone number you enter for a reply is remembered on the phone so that you do not have to type it again.

Location

Location is optional. Your position is processed on the phone: to show where you are, work out distances and guide you along a route. It is not sent to the developer and no history of your movements is kept. The exception is the same: the pin point in a report, which matches your position if you chose “My location” yourself and did not move the pin.

Route navigation, unlike on iPhone, continues with the screen off. While it runs, the app receives your position and a notification with a Stop button is shown. Navigation ends when you tap Stop, or shortly after you arrive. The app does not ask for permanent background location. Voice prompts are spoken by the speech engine installed on your phone.
